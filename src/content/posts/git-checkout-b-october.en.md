September was chaos, personally and professionally.

The personal part I'm keeping to myself, but the professional part I can tell.
I wrapped up the ANFAIA fellowship, the project picked up some press coverage
(and I hope to have more news about it in the coming months), I went to
HackSpain, I started teaching at a tutoring centre and, almost without noticing,
I'm already working alongside GEMEDATA, the startup behind HISTORA.

Somewhere in the middle of all that I realised something I already knew but had
never written down: I'm the kind of person who gets hooked on something
different every month.

In June I was convinced research was my thing. Reading papers, investigating,
discovering and building things, measuring the noise before trusting a number.
Today, in October, what I care about is the startup I've just started working
with getting somewhere.

For a while I thought that meant I didn't know what I wanted. Now I think it's
exactly the opposite: everything that hooks me leaves me something for the next
thing. What I learned doing research this summer is precisely what I'm applying
at HISTORA today. I'm going in eager and excited. I want the company to grow and
to get credit for the work it does, because from the inside you see everything
that goes into it.

Part of the blame for this restlessness goes to ecosystems like Exponential and
itnig. Two months ago I knew nothing about this world (big shoutout to my
brother, who's the one who showed it to me), and now I'm hooked: people betting
their time and their money on building something that didn't exist before. If
I've learned one thing from listening to them, it's that the idea is worth
little. What matters is shipping a product.

But one thing hasn't changed between June and October: I want to go far.
Researching, building product, or whatever December throws at me, what drives me
is seeing how far I'm able to get.

And there has never been a better moment to try. In the agentic world we live
in, a small team can do what used to take a whole department a few years ago.
That doesn't mean it's easy. It means the barrier is no longer having a hundred
people behind you, but being clear about which problem you want to solve and for
whom.

(And if you're asking what's next: the end of 2026 is already loaded with
personal assistants, and more show up every month... Imagine 2027. But that's
another article.)

So yes: every month I open a new branch, and maybe in December I'll be into
something else. But they all come off the same main: getting as far as I can.
